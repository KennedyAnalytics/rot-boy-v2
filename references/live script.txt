If I had 90 days to make real money with AI, I would not build an app.

I would build one machine that makes a business money

and sell that machine over and over again.

Here’s exactly how I’d do it.

Step one: find money that is already leaking.

Do not start with AI.

Start with a business problem.

For this example, let’s use a roofing company.

They spend five thousand dollars on ads.

A homeowner clicks one.

They fill out a quote form.

Name.

Phone number.

Address.

“I think my roof is leaking.”

And then...

nothing.

The lead sits in an inbox for four hours.

By the time somebody calls them back, another roofer already booked the inspection.

That company did not have a lead problem.

They had a follow-up problem.

And that is exactly the kind of problem I want.

Because fixing it has a dollar value.

Step two: turn the problem into an offer.

Do not sell:

“AI automation.”

The business owner does not care.

Sell this:

“I install a system that responds to every new lead immediately, follows up automatically, and helps turn more of the leads you already paid for into booked appointments.”

Now the value is obvious.

Step three: build the actual machine.

A lead comes in.

The system creates a record.

AI reads the message.

Then it classifies what the person wants.

New quote.

Pricing question.

Scheduling.

Existing customer.

Not interested.

Ready to book.

Now we have state.

If it is a new quote—

send the right response.

If they ask about pricing—

answer using the company’s actual rules.

If the question needs a human—

route it to a human.

If they do not reply—

follow up later.

If they say stop—

stop.

And if they are ready to book—

send the calendar.

They pick a time.

The CRM moves them from:

New lead

to:

Appointment booked.

The follow-up sequence shuts off.

And the owner gets a message:

New inspection booked.

That is the product.

Not a chatbot.

Not a demo.

A machine that takes something the business is already paying for—

a lead—

and moves it closer to money.

Step four: make it reliable before you sell it.

Open a spreadsheet.

Create twenty fake leads.

Give each one a different situation.

One wants a quote.

One asks a weird question.

One gives the wrong phone number.

One says they already hired someone.

One wants an appointment tomorrow.

One keeps changing their mind.

Run all twenty through the system.

Watch what happens.

Where does it break?

Where does AI make an assumption?

Where does it need a human?

Fix those cases.

Run them again.

You are not trying to make the AI look impressive.

You are trying to make the service dependable.

That difference matters.

Step five: pick one niche and find 100 businesses.

Do not target every small business in America.

Pick one.

Roofers.

Then find 100 roofing companies already running ads.

Why?

Because they are already spending money to create the thing your system improves.

You are not convincing them leads matter.

They already know.

Now your outreach becomes simple:

“You’re already paying for leads.

I built a system that follows up with the ones your team misses and tries to turn them into booked inspections.

If I showed you how it would work using your business, would you want to see it?”

You do not need 100 yeses.

You need a few calls.

Then one customer.

**Step six: let client number one pay for the machine.**

Maybe they pay $1,500 to install it.

Then $500 a month to keep it running.

The exact number is not the lesson.

The lesson is what happens next.

Client number two comes in.

Do you rebuild everything?

No.

The intake already exists.

The classification already exists.

The follow-up engine already exists.

The booking logic already exists.

You change:

their services,

their rules,

their calendar,

their CRM,

their brand voice.

Then you run the same machine again.

Client three.

Client four.

Client five.

Now the economics change.

The first customer helped pay you to build the system.

Every customer after that is paying you to reuse infrastructure you already own.

That is leverage.

Step seven: use the 90 days correctly.

Month one:

Build one service and make it reliable.

Month two:

Sell that one service until people pay you for it.

Month three:

Standardize onboarding, automate more of delivery, and stack customers onto the same infrastructure.

That is it.

The mistake would be spending those 90 days learning twenty AI tools.

You do not need twenty tools.

You need one expensive problem—

one clear offer—

one reliable machine—

and enough repetition to get good at selling and delivering it.

That is how I would use AI to make real money in the next 90 days.

Not by trying to predict the future.

By finding money that is already leaking—

and building a machine that catches it.
